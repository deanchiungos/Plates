# Plate designs — what the Keegan dataset can and cannot give us

`plate-designs.csv` is generated by `ios/tools/plate_designs.py` from Jon Keegan's
[us-license-plates](https://github.com/jonkeegan/us-license-plates) (8,331 rows,
July 2023 snapshot). Re-run the script rather than editing the CSV by hand.

**75 designs across all 51 US jurisdictions**, down from 8,331:

| kind | rows | what it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `base` | 51 | the current standard design — 37 resolved, **14 absent from the source** |
| `variant` | 12 | another standard design in concurrent issue, different enough to be its own sighting |
| `retro` | 12 | an older design still orderable today: reissues and long-running commemoratives |

## The source is a shopping catalogue, not a design history

This is the finding that matters. Keegan scraped what each agency lets you *order*,
so 5,576 of the 8,331 rows are specialty stock: 2,163 military, 1,342 charity and
club, 964 university, 141 sports team, plus government, occupational and disability
classes.

Two consequences:

**There are no historical designs in it.** Nothing tells you what was on the road in
1985. The closest it gets is the `retro` category — California's Legacy black-and-gold,
Colorado's four Historical colours, six state centennial plates — which are old
designs *still being issued*, not a record of past ones. A real design history needs a
different source.

**Fourteen agencies never list their own standard plate,** because the standard plate
isn't something you order. Maryland publishes 989 plates and not one of them is the
plate on most Maryland cars. Those rows carry the design name with empty image fields
and `NOT IN SOURCE` in `source_page`:

> AR, CT, DC, DE, LA, MD, MN, MT, NC, NE, OK, TX, VT, WA

Nebraska deserves a note: the only "Sandhill Crane" row in the source is a
wildlife-conservation specialty, not the 2023 standard, and an earlier pass of this
curation wrongly took it for the base.

## California is the reason `variant` exists

Five earlier California standards are still legal on the road alongside the current
Sun design — Script, Block, two Blue-and-Golds, Black-and-Gold — and they look
nothing like each other. Any "log the design, not just the state" feature has to
handle a state issuing several standards at once, and California is the proof.

## The agency URLs are rotting

`source_img` points at the state agency. Probing a sample of 13, **four were already
dead** (two connection failures, one 403, one 404) — expected for 2023 links. The
repo's cropped copy worked in all four cases, which is why the CSV carries `repo_img`
as well.

That is exactly backwards from what we want legally: the durable copy is the one
Keegan doesn't own. See the earlier analysis — his repo has no licence at all
(`spdx_id: NOASSERTION`), the images are state agency artwork he downloaded, and he
cannot grant rights he never had. **Treat this CSV as an index to primary sources, not
as an image library.** 1,044 source rows have no agency image URL at all because the
plates were published only inside a PDF; for those the cropped page is the only place
the design can be seen, and `plate_img` is `MISSING.png` where he found nothing.

## Not covered

The app has 65 plates. This dataset covers 51 of them. Missing: Puerto Rico, the 13
Canadian provinces and territories, and the bonus plates.
