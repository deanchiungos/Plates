# Plate database audit

`research/plate-history.csv`, scope `all` — 3958 rows across 65 jurisdictions.

Nothing here is fixed automatically. Every line is a claim about the world that wants a person to check it.

## 1. Timeline conflicts

A jurisdiction's designs should tile its history: each one picking up where the last left off. An **OVERLAP** means two designs claim the same months and at most one of them can be the general issue; a **GAP** means a stretch of road with nothing on it.

Two softer labels keep those from drowning. **VARIANTS** is a design plus rows Wikipedia describes as “as above, but…”, which are meant to run alongside it. **REPEATED SPAN** is the same years listed twice from different rows.

The backticked range is what the parser understood; the quoted string beside it is what Wikipedia actually wrote.

- **Montana (MT), 2010-present** — checked 2026-08-03. Quoted from the Notes on the 2012 rows: "Replicas of 1991, 2000 and 2006 bases and the optional Montana Centennial plate, issued due to mixed reception of the 2010 blue base." Montana deliberately reissued three older designs alongside the 2010 base, so four designs claiming 2012 onwards is right.
- **North Carolina (NC), 2010-present** — checked 2026-08-03. North Carolina offers three standard plates at once and the article labels two of them "Alternative issue": the 2015 quill-pen "First In Freedom" and the 2019 national-flag "IN GOD WE TRUST", both alongside the 2010 Wright Flyer base. Three open-ended rows with visibly different graphics is the correct reading.
- **Ohio (OH), 2004-2013** — checked 2026-08-03. Quoted from the article's own Notes column on the Sunburst row: "Issued concurrently with the 'Beautiful Ohio' base (below) from November 23, 2009." Ohio ran both standard bases side by side for three years, so the overlap is the fact rather than an error.
- **Prince Edward Island (PE), 2007-2012** — checked 2026-08-03. Three serial series sharing one Design cell by rowspan, each with "Issued concurrently with 'Canada's Green Province' base" in its Notes — including a French-language series running against the English one. Same plate, concurrent series.
- **Utah (UT), 2007-2024** — checked 2026-08-03. The Utah table says it repeatedly: "Redesigned Ski base and updated Arch base, issued concurrently", and later simply "Issued concurrently." Utah offers the Delicate Arch and the skier as two standard bases at the same time, and each has had its own die changes, which is why the cluster is eight designs rather than two.
